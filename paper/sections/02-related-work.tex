\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relatedwork}

Prior research relevant to the proposed prototype falls into three
categories: methods for automatic product identification,
sensor-instrumented smart-refrigerator systems, and visual
food-quality analysis. This section reviews each in turn and
positions the contribution of the proposed module against the state
of the art.

\subsection{Product Identification Methods}

\subsubsection{Radio-frequency identification}
RFID-based inventory systems eliminate manual data entry and tolerate
partial occlusion, two characteristics that are attractive for
cabinet-like enclosures~\cite{abhiram2025iotmonitor}. Their practical
limitations are well established. Every monitored item must carry a
tag, a reader must be installed inside the cabinet, and per-tag costs
remain non-trivial. In the Turkish retail context, packaged
refrigerated goods are rarely pre-tagged, which transfers the tagging
burden onto the end user and largely negates the convenience benefit
of the approach.

\subsubsection{Barcode-based identification}
Barcode systems exploit the labels already printed on packaging and
are inexpensive to deploy. They require favorable scan geometry and
deteriorate quickly when packaging surfaces become creased or
condensation-affected~\cite{pelobello2026chickout}. The labels often
end up against the back of the shelf, forcing the user to reposition
items to scan them. As a result, barcode-driven cabinet inventory
systems trend toward abandonment or toward manual rescanning that
recreates the original problem.

\subsubsection{Camera-based recognition}
Camera-based recognition systems identify items from on-shelf imagery
using either generic image classifiers or domain-specific
models~\cite{ericyang2022smartfridge,vadlamudi2025fridgesense}.
Reported accuracies are competitive when sufficient training data are
available, but classifier confidence degrades sharply when fewer than
ten training frames per product are available---an unrealistic
requirement for household-scale deployments. The associated compute
load is also incompatible with low-power microcontrollers without
offloading inference to the cloud.

\subsubsection{QR-based identification}
QR labelling combines the absence of dataset and inference
requirements with the high information density of a printable
two-dimensional code. Decoding is computationally inexpensive and
robust under cabinet illumination. Per-product cost reduces to the
cost of a printed sticker. Table~\ref{tab:identification-methods}
summarises the four families.

\subsection{Sensor-Instrumented Smart Refrigerators}

A second line of related work addresses the instrumentation of the
storage enclosure itself. Goel et al.~\cite{goel2023iotfridge} combine
a small camera with weight sensors to derive shopping
recommendations. Sarkar et al.~\cite{sarkar2024proactive} extend this
approach with consumption-pattern-driven proactive notifications.
Shetty and Shetty~\cite{shetty2024monitoring} report an IoT-monitored
food-storage prototype centred on environmental sensing, while Nandini
et al.~\cite{nandini2023userconvenience} target usability improvements
to the refrigerator interface itself.

The common pattern across this body of work is single-modality
optimisation. Combinations of weight tracking, environmental sensing,
gas-trend observation, and automatic product identification within a
single low-cost reference design are
rare~\cite{sarawathi2025spoilage}, and the present work is positioned
explicitly to fill that gap.

\subsection{Visual Food-Quality Analysis}

A third strand of related work focuses on visual indicators of food
spoilage. Banana browning is a common benchmark because the surface
colour of bananas evolves predictably with ripeness. The dominant
approach in recent literature uses convolutional neural networks with
transfer learning from large-scale visual
backbones~\cite{vadlamudi2025fridgesense}. CNN-based detectors reach
high accuracy on representative test sets at the cost of significant
training data, edge-side compute, or a cloud round-trip.

In contrast, the present work uses a pixel-level RGB classifier that
runs in a few milliseconds on commodity smartphone hardware and
reports a single interpretable percentage. The classifier is intended
as a reference baseline against which model-based detectors can be
compared, and as a feasibility check for cabinet deployments that
cannot guarantee a network connection.

\subsection{Positioning of the Proposed Module}

The proposed prototype differs from prior art on four axes:
(i) it integrates four sensing modalities rather than optimising a
single one;
(ii) it adopts QR-based product registration, which avoids the
hardware overhead of RFID and the geometry constraints of barcodes;
(iii) it exposes real-time visibility through a cross-platform mobile
dashboard backed by a small Python bridge, eliminating the need for a
Wi-Fi-capable sensor microcontroller; and
(iv) it provides a transparent, training-free ripeness baseline
suitable for reproducibility studies. The detailed system design
follows in Section~\ref{sec:systemdesign}.
