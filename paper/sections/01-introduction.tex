\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Household food waste constitutes the largest single contributor to
global food losses. According to the United Nations Environment
Programme, household kitchens account for more than 60\,\% of the
estimated 931 million tonnes of food wasted worldwide every year, with
a direct economic loss exceeding one trillion US dollars
annually~\cite{unep2024foodwaste}. Beyond financial cost, food that
reaches the household refrigerator and is later discarded carries
embedded environmental costs from production, processing, transport,
and cooling, none of which are recovered.

The reasons for refrigerator-stage waste are well documented:
consumers lose visibility of items already in storage, misremember
expiration dates, and detect spoilage only after visible deterioration
has occurred. Although manual logging applications attempt to address
these patterns, their adoption is constrained by the discipline they
require from the user~\cite{nandini2023userconvenience}.

A number of automated approaches have been proposed in the literature
to reduce this dependence on user discipline. Camera-based product
recognition systems~\cite{ericyang2022smartfridge,vadlamudi2025fridgesense}
identify items from on-shelf imagery using trained classifiers, but
the required datasets are not readily available for regional product
catalogues and inference loads are unsuitable for low-power edge
deployment. Radio-frequency identification (RFID) systems eliminate
manual entry at the cost of per-product tags and an in-cabinet
reader~\cite{abhiram2025iotmonitor}. Barcode systems are inexpensive
but assume favorable scan geometry and remain
manual~\cite{pelobello2026chickout}. Most recent prototypes optimise a
single sensing modality~\cite{sarawathi2025spoilage,sarkar2024proactive},
limiting the operator's view of the cabinet to a partial picture.

This paper introduces a cabinet-scale prototype that fuses four
complementary sensing modalities---weight, temperature and humidity,
gas trend, and visual---inside a single low-cost integrated platform.
An Arduino Uno coordinates the load-cell, DHT11, and MQ135 channels;
an AI Thinker ESP32-CAM operates as an independent Wi-Fi camera node
that exposes JPEG capture and MJPEG streaming endpoints; a small
Python USB-to-HTTP bridge translates the Arduino's serial output into
a CORS-enabled HTTP feed; and a cross-platform Flutter application
provides dashboard, camera, product, alert, and configuration views.
QR-coded stickers attached to packaged items are decoded by a tiled
multi-detection routine that recovers several stickers from a single
camera frame, and a pixel-level RGB classifier produces an
interpretable estimate of banana surface spotting.

The contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A multi-modal cabinet prototype that combines load-cell
        weighing, DHT11-based environmental sensing, MQ135 gas-trend
        observation, ESP32-CAM-based QR registration, and LED-assisted
        illumination using off-the-shelf components within a single
        low-cost integrated platform.
  \item A Python USB-to-HTTP bridge that decouples the sensor node
        from the wireless network and removes the requirement for a
        Wi-Fi-capable microcontroller on the sensor side.
  \item A cross-platform Flutter dashboard targeting Android and the
        web, with five integrated screens and runtime calibration
        controls accessed through the bridge.
  \item A tiled multi-QR recognition routine that recovers several
        product codes from a single camera capture, together with an
        interpretable pixel-level RGB classifier for banana surface
        spotting.
  \item An openly archived implementation, mirrored in the Software
        Heritage Archive with a persistent identifier, that supports
        independent reproduction of the experiments reported in
        Section~\ref{sec:results}.
\end{itemize}
